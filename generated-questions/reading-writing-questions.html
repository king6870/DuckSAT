It is more accurate to describe it as a record of where to look, a map pointing to information rather than a replay of the information itself. In this sense, memory stored externally and memory stored internally do not play identical roles in cognition: the former acts as an external scaffold that supports remembrance, while the latter remains the internal construction that one experiences as knowing.
