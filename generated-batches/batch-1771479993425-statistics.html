Question 3 EASY
Two fair coins are flipped. What is the probability that at least one heads appears? Use the complement: $P(\text{at least one H}) = 1 - P(\text{TT}) = 1 - (\tfrac{1}{2})^2$.
  • A) $\tfrac{1}{4}$
  • B) $\tfrac{1}{2}$
  • C) $\tfrac{3}{4}$
  • D) $1$
Explanation:
Possible outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT (4 equally likely). At least one heads occurs in 3 outcomes, so $P(\text{at least one H}) = \frac{3}{4}$. Using the complement confirms: $1 - (\tfrac{1}{2})^2 = 1 - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{3}{4}$.

From a standard deck of 52 cards, what is the probability of drawing at least one ace in two draws without replacement?
  • A) 33/221
  • B) 1/6
  • C) 2/13
  • D) 0.130
Explanation:
Step 1: It is easier to compute the complement: P(at least one ace) = 1 − P(no ace in both draws). Step 2: P(no ace) for two draws without replacement = (48/52) × (47/51). Step 3: This equals (12/13) × (47/51) = 564/663 = 188/221. Step 4: Therefore P(at least one ace) = 1 − 188/221 = 33/221 ≈ 0.1493. The correct option is A, 33/221. $$
