Answer: A) $9$

Step-by-Step Explanation:

Let $A = (0,0)$, $B = (6,0)$, and $P = (2,3)$. Since $A$ and $B$ lie on the x-axis, the base of triangle $ABP$ can be taken as $|AB|$, and the height is the perpendicular distance from $P$ to the line $AB$, which in this case is simply the absolute value of the $y$-coordinate of $P$, i.e. $|y_P|$. Compute the base: $|AB| = \sqrt{(6-0)^2 + (0-0)^2} = \sqrt{36} = 6$. Height: $\text{height} = |y_P| = |3| = 3$. Area: $[ABP] = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot |AB| \cdot \text{height} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 3 = 9$. (Alternative method: using the determinant formula for area with coordinates, $[ABP] = \dfrac{1}{2} \left| \det(\overrightarrow{AB}, \overrightarrow{AP}) \right|$, where $\overrightarrow{AB} = (6,0)$ and $\overrightarrow{AP} = (2,3)$; thus $[ABP] = \dfrac{1}{2} |6\cdot 3 - 0\cdot 2| = 9$.)
